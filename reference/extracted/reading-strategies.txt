1. Multiple Choice – Single answer
=== slide 4 ===
MCQ – Single answer
Scoring: 1 for correct answer  -  0 for incorrect answer
Number of Questions: 2 – 3
Recommended time: 1 – 1.5 per question
Individual timer: none – shares  23 – 30 mins section timer)
Load: only worth 3% of your Reading score,
The strategy: Score this quickly and move on. Don't spend 4 minutes on a 3% task when Fill in the Blanks (Dropdown) at 25% is waiting.
=== slide 5 ===
Strategy
Tip #1: Read the question first. Know what you're looking for before you read the passage. This transforms your reading from aimless to targeted.
Tip #2: Eliminate Clearly Wrong Answers
skim the answer options and eliminate any that are clearly wrong, options that:
Contradict information in the passage
Introduce ideas not mentioned in the passage
Are partially correct but distort the key detail
Tip #3: Watch for Absolute Language: Wrong answer options frequently use absolute quantifiers:
"The study PROVED that…“  /  "ALL participants agreed…“  /"The author ALWAYS…“
Tip #4: The Correct Answer Is Usually Directly Supported by the Passage
Tip #5: Don't Let Long Passages Slow You Down: don't read every word, Skim and scan!

2. Multiple Choice – Multiple answers
=== slide 7 ===
MCQ – Multiple answer
Scoring:  +1 for correct answer  /  -1 for incorrect answer / 0 for no selection
Your scoring cannot fall below zero
Number of Questions: 2 – 3
Recommended time: 1.5 – 2 minutes per question
Individual timer: none – ( shares  23 – 30 mins section timer)
Load: moderate score worth 3%-10% of your Reading score, depends on questions number
The strategy: Use Safe Selection (Avoid Blind Guessing)
=== slide 8 ===
Strategy
Read the question first: identify the keywords to grasp the question requirements and the meaning of all the options.
Scan and skim the questions to find keywords
Eliminate the wrong answers
Check Each Option, One by One: Go through the list of options. Treat each one like a true/false question.
Look out for traps:
The text might say: “The company’s profits grew by 30% last year.”
A tricky option might say: “The company’s employees received large bonuses last year.”
The text might say: “Some research suggests a link between…”
A tricky option might say: “It is always the case that there is a link between…”
Watch out for words like all, every, never, and only. They often turn a right idea into a wrong answer.
Only click on an answer if you are 100% certain it is correct.

3. Reorder paragraph
=== slide 10 ===
Reorder Paragraphs
Partial Credit via Adjacent Pairs: You don’t need entire sequence to be 100% in order to earn points.
Point Allocation: Points are awarded for correctly paired adjacent sentences (e.g., if sentence B is placed directly after sentence A and that is correct, you get 1 point for the A–B pair).
Left Panel: Contains 4 to 5 jumbled text boxes.
Right Panel: An empty target area where you drop or move the sentences.
Controls: drag and drop sentences with your mouse or use up/down arrow buttons to sequence them
No Negative Marking: so you should never leave a Re-order Paragraph question blank.
Number of Questions: 2 – 3
Recommended time: you should spend about 1 to 2/3 minutes per question
Load: moderate score worth 9%-10% of your Reading score, depends on questions number
The strategy: Use Safe Selection (Avoid Blind Guessing)
=== slide 11 ===
Find the Independent first Topic Sentence: Look for a broad statement that introduces a core subject or concept without relying on prior context.
This is the sentence that will have the overall topic idea/statement within it. The topic sentence can stand alone;
Eliminate sentences starting with pronouns (he, she, they, this, these) because they point to a noun introduced earlier.
Eliminate sentences starting with transition or linking words (e.g., however, therefore, thus, furthermore, moreover)
Check for definite articles (the) versus indefinite articles (a/an): initial sentences often introduce a noun generally with a or an before referencing it specifically with the later.
Match Nouns and Pronouns
Identify full noun names or subjects first (e.g., the first musical keyboard, Dr. John, ants), then scan for matching pronouns (it, he, they) in subsequent sentences.
Sentence 1: The researchers collected data from several cities.
Sentence 2: This data provided valuable insights into urban planning.

=== slide 12 ===
Re-order paragraphs
Apply the General to Specific Rule:  Sentences in well-organized paragraphs usually follow a natural progression, starting with a broad or general statement, which is then narrowed down by specific details or examples.
General statement: She conducted various studies on emotional expression in actors.
Specific details: In one study, she asked actors to assess their emotions while performing.
Follow the Sequence of Events: In many cases, the sentences in a paragraph describe events that occur in a logical, chronological order. Recognizing this order can provide key insights into how the sentences should be arranged.
Sentence 1: The team first conducted interviews with local residents.
Sentence 2: Next, they analyzed the survey results to determine patterns in the data.
Sentence 3: Finally, they published their findings in a well-regarded journal.

=== slide 13 ===
Re-order paragraphs
Look for Linking Words: These words help establish the logical flow of ideas, making them excellent clues
Addition: Furthermore, moreover, in addition, also
Contrast: However, on the other hand, nevertheless
Cause and effect: Therefore, thus, consequently, as a result
Time sequence: First, next, then, finally
Read Through and Verify
Once arranged, read the entire block from start to finish to test coherence and natural flow.
Do not waste excessive time stuck on a single difficult item; complete your best guess and move on to protect your overall time.

Strategies
Tip #1: Read the Full Passage First Same principle as the Dropdown version: read the whole passage before placing any words. The meaning of later sentences can clarify the right word for an earlier blank.
Give yourself 30-45 seconds to read the full passage before touching the word bank.
Tip #2: Identify the Part of Speech First Before looking at the words, decide what type of word goes in this blank?
Is it a noun, verb, adjective, adverb? Then look at the word bank and filter out words of the wrong part of speech.
Ex: "The study showed that the participants _______ significantly more stress after the intervention."
Type needed: verb (past tense, to fit "The participants ___") Looking at the word bank, you can eliminate all nouns, adjectives, and adverbs.
Tip #3: Use Collocations:.
Example: "The company _______ a major decision regarding…"
Options: "made" and "did" (both verbs, both grammatically possible)
Correct collocation: "made a decision" (not "did a decision")
Tip #4: No Negative Marking, Always Place Something
